What it is

Who it's for: CBAM consultants, trade-compliance advisers, analysts and importers who need to decide which supplier plants to ask for real emissions data first.

What it saves you: matching plants to the right default value by hand. The Commission's default table has over 100 country sheets, "Other countries" fallbacks, route letters and per-product codes; Climate TRACE has plants but no CBAM product codes. This pack has done that join for 3,110 plants in 117 countries.

What you see per plant: location, production route, capacity, a modelled emissions intensity (Climate TRACE), the official default for its country and CN code (with 2026–2028 mark-ups), the gap between the two, and a confidence level for that gap. Of 910 steel plants, 94 have a high-confidence and 695 a medium-confidence gap; of 2,009 cement plants, 800 high and 1,209 medium. Every gap is a modelled screening estimate; read it with its confidence level and note.

Each pack is a zip with data.xlsx (installations, defaults per CN code, data dictionary, sources), data.csv, defaults.csv, data.gpkg (GeoPackage, WGS84), README.html and LICENCE.md.

Aluminium appears only in the all-sectors and country packs, with defaults, routes and capacity but no gap, because the Climate TRACE aluminium figure includes power emissions and isn't comparable with the CBAM direct default. Russian and Belarusian plants are in the global packs, flagged for EU import restrictions.

The four confidence levels

  • High: the plant's route matches the route behind the default, the intensity is plausible, and the factor is plant-specific.
  • Medium: informative, with a caveat in gap_note — for example a generic Climate TRACE factor shared by 20+ plants in the country, or a route that differs from the default's.
  • Low: implausible intensity, or a route mismatch against an "Other countries" default.
  • Withheld: no gap given (e.g. induction furnaces, all aluminium); the default is still shown.

These describe confidence in a modelled estimate, never verified emissions.

What it is not

  • Not verified, reported or actual emissions, and not "embedded emissions" in the CBAM sense.
  • Not for use in CBAM declarations: importers must use verified supplier data or the official defaults.
  • Not a list of EU exporters: nothing says a plant sells to the EU.
  • Purchased inputs (pig iron, DRI, electricity) aren't modelled.
  • Default values are reproduced for convenience; the regulation prevails.
