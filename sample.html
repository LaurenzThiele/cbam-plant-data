How to read the gap

Gap = (EU default - modelled intensity) / EU default; positive means the official default is above the plant's modelled intensity. It is a modelled screening estimate; the confidence level says how far to trust it and never means the emissions are verified. Steel and aluminium defaults count direct emissions only; cement compares total with total. Empty gap = withheld.

  • high: The plant's production route matches the route the EU default is based on, the modelled intensity is within a plausible range for that route, and Climate TRACE uses plant-specific rather than generic numbers. Read the gap as a reasonable first indication.
  • medium: The gap is informative but carries a caveat, spelled out in gap_note: for example Climate TRACE applies the same generic factor to all similar plants in the country (the gap then shows how far the default sits above a typical plant, not this plant's own performance), or the plant's route differs from the default's benchmark route and purchased inputs such as pig iron or DRI are not modelled.
  • low: A weak signal. The modelled intensity looks implausible for the plant's route, or the plant's route differs from the default's route and the default is the generic 'Other countries and territories' value. Use it to decide where to look, not as a finding.
  • withheld: No gap is given because a comparison would mislead (for example induction-furnace plants, whose emission factor Climate TRACE does not document, or aluminium, where scope is not confirmed). The official default, route and capacity are still shown.

Method

Installations, routes, capacity and modelled emissions: Climate TRACE. Defaults: official Commission table (country x CN code; the 'Other countries and territories' value where the country has none). Route is read from Climate TRACE's route field, never inferred. Columns from sources without a confirmed licence are left out.
